## A comparison brief, not a scoreboard

Cupping notes are usually read one coffee at a time. The useful decisions, however, happen across a table: which lot has the clearest acidity, which one stays balanced, and whether a higher score still makes sense once price enters the frame. Coffee Cupping reorganizes that discussion into one linked browser workspace.

The interface uses five representative source lots and six sensory metrics. It keeps the original score visible, but avoids declaring a universal “best” coffee. Selection is a reading aid: choose a lot once and the matrix, profile, and value point follow together.

## One matrix, three linked views

The matrix is the product’s stable spine. Rows hold the sensory vocabulary and columns hold the lots, so a reader can scan either dimension without changing context. The surrounding profile and scatter views answer different questions while sharing the same selection.

Absolute mode is for reading what the cupping sheet recorded. Delta mode subtracts the room average for each metric, leveling the columns around a common zero line. This makes a coffee’s relative shape easier to see without replacing its source score.

## Price and quality in the same frame

Sensory score and green coffee price describe different things. Placing them together makes that tradeoff inspectable without collapsing it into a recommendation. A less expensive lot can be read for reliability and body; a lot with a higher score can be evaluated against the premium it asks the buyer to carry.

The view deliberately stops short of purchasing guidance. Freight, contract size, availability, roast development, and the needs of a particular menu all sit outside this sample dataset.

## Outcome

The completed study turns a dense cupping table into a compact comparison sequence: establish the source score, normalize against the room, then inspect quality alongside price. The linked selection keeps those steps coherent instead of asking the reader to reconcile separate charts by eye.

It is a working interface study, not a measured business result. The useful part is the interaction model: choose once, read the data several ways, and leave the underlying numbers unchanged.
