## A catalogue is an information problem

The private build began with a familiar commerce tension: a stationery shop may feel visually simple while its catalogue is not. Paper sizes, ruling, colours, nib widths, refills, materials, makers, and stock states all need a stable structure before the storefront can feel calm.

The design organizes those variables into a short path from collection to product to cart. Photography carries the tactile impression; taxonomy, availability, and consistent product data do the quieter work of helping a shopper decide.

## Collection structure before decoration

Collections separate paper, writing tools, desk objects, and replenishment items, while storefront filters expose attributes that change a buying decision. Product type, availability, colour, and size are modelled as data instead of being buried in descriptions.

## A theme editors can keep using

The implementation follows Shopify’s Online Store 2.0 architecture: Liquid renders the storefront, JSON templates choose the page structure, and reusable sections and blocks keep merchandising decisions available in the theme editor. Dynamic sources connect block settings to product and collection data rather than duplicating content inside a template.

The system keeps responsibilities narrow. Templates establish the page type, sections define reusable merchandising modules, blocks provide reorderable content, and snippets handle small repeated patterns such as prices, badges, and product media.

## Options, variants, and product truth

A product page has to distinguish descriptive attributes from purchasable variants. Format and colour can resolve to a specific variant with its own availability, price, and media; care details and material notes can live in metafields without multiplying theme code.

Quantity controls and cart forms are presented only when the selected variant can be purchased. The public case study does not reproduce the private shop’s products or operational data; it documents the system decisions behind a typical product state.

## A short, legible handoff to checkout

Cart state keeps product identity, selected option, quantity, and price visible before Shopify takes over the secure checkout flow. The page avoids inventing a second checkout model inside the theme and makes shipping uncertainty explicit before the handoff.

## Responsive delivery and outcome

Product media reserves its aspect ratio before loading, collection grids collapse without changing reading order, and option labels remain available to keyboard and screen-reader users. The visual layer stays deliberately restrained so product differences remain more prominent than interface chrome.

The result is a reusable commerce foundation: catalogue structure can grow, merchants can rearrange approved modules, and the route from discovery to cart remains consistent. This is an implementation outcome, not a claim about conversion, revenue, or measured business performance.
