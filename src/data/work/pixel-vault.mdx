## Discovery before exchange mechanics

Pixel Vault was an interface concept for an NFT marketplace. The design begins with visual discovery—collections, artwork, and creators—before introducing price, bids, auction timing, wallet access, or account actions.

The homepage treats the marketplace as a sequence of increasingly specific decisions. A visitor first understands the catalogue, then compares items, then reads creator and transaction context. The darker interface keeps the varied artwork prominent while using a restrained violet accent for actions and state.

## Collections create the first map

The primary navigation separates home, exploration, wallet, company information, and contact. Below it, collection groupings create a broader map of the inventory before the visitor reaches individual assets.

Each collection combines artwork with a creator identity and item count. That structure gives discovery a clearer starting point than presenting every listing in one undifferentiated grid.

## Listings keep market state attached to the artwork

Individual cards combine the artwork, creator handle, item title, price, highest bid, and—when relevant—auction time. The repeated structure supports scanning without allowing the transactional data to overwhelm the image.

## Creator and wallet context clarify roles

Creator cards make the people behind the listings visible and provide a route beyond the item grid. Wallet and account affordances remain present in the navigation so the visitor can understand the intended relationship between browsing, identity, and exchange without turning the entire interface into a transaction terminal.

These controls document the intended product model only. The preserved snapshot does not create accounts, connect wallets, verify ownership, mint tokens, submit bids, transfer assets, or execute blockchain transactions.

## Supporting questions and exits

FAQ, contact, newsletter, and footer areas give the longer marketplace page a clear ending. They acknowledge the trust and onboarding questions that come with a product built around transactions, while preserving routes back to exploration, creators, wallets, and company information.

## Sunset and preservation

Pixel Vault is retained as a record of a 2023 marketplace interface study, not as an active exchange. The work demonstrates catalogue hierarchy, reusable listing structures, creator visibility, responsive thinking, and the placement of wallet and bid cues within a broader discovery experience.

No adoption, sales, transaction volume, conversion, token performance, creator income, or business result is claimed. The preserved URL may remain useful as a visual snapshot, but its decommissioned status is displayed wherever the project appears in the portfolio.
