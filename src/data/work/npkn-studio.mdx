## A studio brief where the work comes first

NPKN describes itself as a visual communication studio in Coahuila, enthusiastic about functionality and innovation in aesthetics. The website study translates that position into a simple rule: the interface should organize the work without competing with it.

The implementation is intentionally quiet. A compact wordmark, direct navigation, and a pale page field establish the frame; color, texture, motion, and illustration come from each project. This is a website study and implementation, not a claim about a client relationship or a measured commercial outcome.

## A modular grid with restrained type

The grid creates hierarchy through scale rather than chrome. A lead project occupies the full first row; the portrait study is balanced by two stacked identity images, followed by a final paired row. Captions remain directly visible beneath every image, so the archive needs no hover behavior or outbound navigation.

Typography plays the opposite role from the imagery. The navigation is compact, labels stay short, and no oversized headline interrupts the project sequence. That restraint gives very different image crops a consistent editorial rhythm.

## Responsive behavior

On narrow screens the archive becomes a single reading column. The header stays economical, touch targets remain reachable, and images retain enough height to act as project introductions rather than thumbnails. The crop changes with the viewport; the order and project identity do not.

## Outcome

The study resolves into a durable portfolio system: a small navigational frame, a flexible image grid, and project captions that remain available to pointer and keyboard users. It gives the visible work enough room to establish its own tone while the website retains a consistent structure.

No performance uplift, client result, or authorship of the underlying studio projects is implied. The work documented here is the website study and its responsive behavior in the browser.
