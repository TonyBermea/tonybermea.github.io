## Editorial density without a feed

Soul Mag explores how a digital issue can feel composed rather than endlessly accumulated. The concept avoids a conventional stack of cards and divides the screen into distinct reading zones: permanent navigation, an issue masthead, a pinned feature, a moving news line, and a ranked article index.

The layout asks the reader to understand the publication spatially. Large type establishes the issue, stable columns preserve orientation, and smaller changes in scale separate features from summaries without adding unnecessary interface chrome.

## The masthead establishes the issue

The oversized working masthead, issue number, and dateline do more than brand the page. Together they create a fixed editorial frame around content that can move independently. The reader always knows which issue is open even when the central story or ranked list changes.

Navigation stays narrow and persistent at the left edge. It is deliberately quieter than the publication title, leaving the issue itself as the strongest piece of wayfinding.

## One story carries the first reading

The pinned feature receives the broadest column, the largest image, and the fullest excerpt. Title, byline, reading time, and summary sit close enough to scan as one unit while maintaining the visual cadence of a print spread.

## Independent columns create pace

The centre story rail and the featured index on the right scroll independently inside a stable page frame. That separation allows the feature to change without displacing navigation, the issue number, or the masthead. A narrow ticker introduces another reading speed: ambient and continuous rather than selected.

This is an interaction model built from overflow and hierarchy rather than elaborate animation. Movement is contained within the part of the page that changes, preserving the broader composition.

## Prototype outcome

The front page supports several ways of reading: pause on the pinned feature, scan the numbered index, follow the ticker, or move through the central stories. What works best is the separation between the stable issue frame and the stories that change inside it.

The preserved build is a design prototype rather than a production publication. Its navigation and article links are placeholders, and it does not include a CMS, account system, article pages, analytics, or measured readership results.
